I tested 4 different settings:
1. Normal panel placement 4.5cm in front of error mic
2. Conventional speaker diagonal to the side, mic slightly moved and turned toward the speaker
3. Panel driven with that new mic location
4. Conventional speaker 4.5cm in front of the mic with panel behind it

Of these, the only 2 that did well were the normal setup and the conventional speaker off to the side.
The latter did slightly better than the former, and was slightly more consistent.

The panel with the moved mic was consistent, although it did worse.
Moving the mic further added extra delay, which in the past we have measured worse performance from.
However, the conventional speaker off to the side had even more delay than that and did fine, so perhaps there is another cause.

The conventional speaker directly in front of the panel did awful, and was very very inconsistent.
This seems to suggest that there is something new introduced when the primary reflections are off of the driven cancellation speaker.
The conventional speaker is likely worse for this than the panel because the cone shape and the grille probably add extra weird distortions.

I didn't try with a panel off to the side though, so we don't have a direct comparison of panel in front vs panel to the side though.
That seems like a logical next step to try and isolate the effect.
